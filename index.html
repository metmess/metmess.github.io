The 6th edition comes to Kharagpur

The Department of Geology & Geophysics, IIT Kharagpur, in association with the Physical Research Laboratory, Ahmedabad, is organising MetMeSS 2026: a planetary science symposium on the origin and evolution of the Solar System.

The programme spans laboratory analysis of Earth and planetary materials alongside the modelling work that deciphers how planetary bodies evolve. Two days of talks, posters and discussion, bracketed by a two-day school for students and a two-day field trip.

Themes & sessions

Abstracts are invited across six sessions. Indicate your preferred session when you submit.

1

Early solar system processes & organics

Presolar grains, IDPs, chronology and the first solids of the Solar System.

2

Differentiated bodies

Asteroids, the Moon and Mars. Differentiation, magmatism and planetary evolution.

3

Asteroids, comets, micrometeorites & returned samples

Small-body science and material brought home by sample-return missions.

4

Planetary analogues

Terrestrial analogue sites and what they teach us about planetary processes.

5

Remote sensing & instrumentation

Orbital and in-situ observation, payload design and data analysis.

6

Space weathering, meteors & the atmosphere

Surface processing of airless bodies, and the role of meteors in atmospheric and ionospheric processes.

Venue, travel & stay

IIT Kharagpur

Founded in 1951 as the first of the Indian Institutes of Technology, IIT Kharagpur hosts the Department of Geology & Geophysics, among the oldest earth science departments in the country. The symposium is held on the main campus in Kharagpur, West Bengal.

Reaching Kharagpur

  • By air. Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport, Kolkata (about 130 km), then road or rail.
  • By rail. Kharagpur Junction is a major stop on the Howrah to Chennai line, about 2 hours from Howrah.
  • By road. NH-16 connects Kharagpur to Kolkata and Bhubaneswar.

Code of conduct

MetMeSS is committed to a welcoming, harassment-free environment for every participant, regardless of background, career stage or identity.

  • Integrity first. Honesty and ethical conduct in research and in scientific discourse are non-negotiable.
  • Respect for difference. Treat every participant with professionalism and respect. Harassment, intimidation and abuse of power will not be tolerated.
  • Strive for excellence. We hold to the highest standards of scientific inquiry and open knowledge-sharing.
Concerns during the symposium should be raised with any member of the organising committee, or by email to the convenors. Reports are handled confidentially.
